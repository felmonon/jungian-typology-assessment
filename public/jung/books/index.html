The Jung Library

Reading guides to Jung’s books

15 short guides. Each says what the book is, who it is for, which parts to read first and what to read next. If you are not sure where to begin, use the reading paths.

Start here

Written for general readers, or short enough to read in a weekend.

Start here · 1962 Memories, Dreams, Reflections Anyone who wants to know who Jung was before learning what he thought. It makes the theory make sense, because it shows what the theory was built to explain. Start here · 1964 Man and His Symbols The best starting point for most people. It assumes no background and was written to be understood. Start here · 1933 Modern Man in Search of a Soul Readers who prefer essays to a system, and anyone curious about Jung’s view of meaning, religion and midlife. Start here · 1957 The Undiscovered Self Readers interested in Jung on politics, conformity and religion, and anyone who wants his late thought in a hundred pages.

Core works

The books where the theory is stated. Read after an introduction.

Core works · 1971 The Portable Jung Readers who have had an introduction and now want primary texts without buying a shelf of volumes. Core works · 1917/1928 Two Essays on Analytical Psychology Readers who want the theory in Jung’s own words and in order. It is widely regarded as the best systematic introduction he wrote. Core works · 1921 Psychological Types Anyone who came to Jung through personality types and wants to see what he actually wrote. Start with Chapter X. Core works · 1934-1954 The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious Readers who know the outline of Jung’s psychology and want the primary texts on archetypes. Read two or three essays first, not the whole volume. Core works · 1952 Answer to Job Readers interested in Jung on religion, evil and the opposites. It is short and passionate, and it offends some readers. Core works · 1952 Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle Readers who want to know what Jung actually claimed about synchronicity. Start with the lecture “On Synchronicity”.

Deep water

Long, late or difficult. Best read in parts, with a guide.

Deep water · 1951 Aion: Researches into the Phenomenology of the Self Everyone should read the first four chapters. The remainder is for readers with a strong interest in religion and symbolism. Deep water · 1912/1952 Symbols of Transformation Readers interested in mythology and in the origins of Jung’s ideas. It is not a first book, and it is not his clearest. Deep water · 1944 Psychology and Alchemy Readers who have the basics and are curious about why alchemy mattered so much to Jung. The introduction can be read by anyone. Deep water · 2009 The Red Book: Liber Novus Readers who already know Jung’s ideas and want to see where they came from. It is a hard and strange book, and a poor introduction. Deep water · 1955-56 Mysterium Coniunctionis Committed readers who have read Psychology and Alchemy. Others can read its final chapter, which stands partly alone.

Sources and method

Quotations on this page were checked against published sources and are cited to the work, and to the paragraph (§) of the Collected Works where that could be confirmed. The summaries are ours. Publication facts were checked against library and reference sources. This page is educational. It is not psychological advice or treatment.
