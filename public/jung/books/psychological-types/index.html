Psychological Types

What it is

The book in which Jung introduced introversion and extraversion and the four functions. It is volume 6 of the Collected Works and the source of every later type system. Most of its length is a history of the type problem in philosophy and literature. The theory itself occupies the last two chapters.

Who it is for. Anyone who came to Jung through personality types and wants to see what he actually wrote. Start with Chapter X.

What is in it

The book appeared in German in 1921 and in English in 1923. Its first nine chapters trace the opposition of two attitudes through the history of thought: early Christian theology, Schiller’s letters on aesthetic education, Nietzsche’s Apollonian and Dionysian, a long study of Carl Spitteler’s epic Prometheus and Epimetheus, psychiatry, aesthetics, and William James’s tough-minded and tender-minded thinkers.

Chapter X, “General Description of the Types”, then describes each of the eight types: the four functions in their extraverted and introverted forms, with the unconscious side of each. Chapter XI, “Definitions”, is a lexicon of about sixty of his terms, from abstraction to will, and includes his fullest definitions of symbol, individuation and the Self.

How to read it

Read Chapter X first. It is under a hundred pages and can be read alone. Use Chapter XI as a reference. The historical chapters are for readers who enjoy the history of ideas, and the one on Schiller and the one on Spitteler contain important passages on the reconciling symbol. The short papers in the appendix, especially “Psychological Typology” from 1936, give his later and simpler statement of the theory.

What it leads to

Readers who know the sixteen four-letter types are often surprised. There are no codes and no questionnaire. Jung describes pure types as extremes that rarely occur, and spends as much time on what each type represses as on what it does well. That emphasis on the inferior function is the bridge from typology to the rest of his psychology, which the Two Essays sets out.

What to read next

Core works · 1917/1928 Two Essays on Analytical Psychology Readers who want the theory in Jung’s own words and in order. It is widely regarded as the best systematic introduction he wrote. Start here · 1964 Man and His Symbols The best starting point for most people. It assumes no background and was written to be understood. Core works · 1934-1954 The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious Readers who know the outline of Jung’s psychology and want the primary texts on archetypes. Read two or three essays first, not the whole volume.

Questions people ask

Do I need to read all of Psychological Types?

No. Chapter X contains the descriptions of the types and can be read by itself. Chapter XI is a glossary to consult. The first nine chapters are historical background.

Is the Myers-Briggs test in Psychological Types?

No. The book has no test and no four-letter codes. The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator was created later by Katharine Briggs and Isabel Briggs Myers, using Jung’s book as a starting point.
