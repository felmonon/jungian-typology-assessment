Reading guide · Deep water · 1951

Aion: Researches into the Phenomenology of the Self

What it is

Volume 9, part 2 of the Collected Works. Its first four chapters are Jung’s shortest and most exact statements on the ego, the shadow, the anima and animus, and the Self. The rest is a difficult study of the Self as it appears in Christian, Gnostic and alchemical symbolism over two thousand years.

Who it is for. Everyone should read the first four chapters. The remainder is for readers with a strong interest in religion and symbolism.

What is in it

Jung wrote Aion in his mid-seventies and published it in 1951. The opening chapters, each only a few pages, define the ego, the shadow, the syzygy of anima and animus, and the Self. They are the late, distilled form of ideas he had worked on for thirty years, and they are where many of his best known sentences come from.

From Chapter V, “Christ, a Symbol of the Self”, the book changes character. Jung argues that the figure of Christ carried the image of the Self for the Western world but left out its dark side, which returned as the Antichrist. He follows this split through the symbolism of the fish, the astrological age of Pisces, Gnostic texts and alchemy, and closes with a formal model of the Self’s structure.

How to read it

Read Chapters I to IV, about thirty-five pages, and treat them as a complete short work. Chapter V is worth the effort for his view of good and evil and the tension of opposites. Most readers can stop there. The middle chapters assume familiarity with early Christian history, astrology and Latin alchemy, and even specialists find them hard.

What it leads to

The first four chapters pair naturally with the second of the Two Essays, which tells the same story at greater length with cases. The religious argument is continued, with more feeling and less scholarship, in Answer to Job, written immediately afterward. The alchemical side is completed in Mysterium Coniunctionis.

What to read next

Core works · 1952 Answer to Job Readers interested in Jung on religion, evil and the opposites. It is short and passionate, and it offends some readers. Core works · 1917/1928 Two Essays on Analytical Psychology Readers who want the theory in Jung’s own words and in order. It is widely regarded as the best systematic introduction he wrote. Deep water · 1955-56 Mysterium Coniunctionis Committed readers who have read Psychology and Alchemy. Others can read its final chapter, which stands partly alone.

Questions people ask

Which chapters of Aion should I read?

The first four: “The Ego”, “The Shadow”, “The Syzygy: Anima and Animus” and “The Self”. They are short and self-contained. Add Chapter V if you want his view of Christ as a symbol of the Self.

What does the title Aion mean?

Aion is a Greek word for an age or era. The book is concerned with the Christian aeon and with how the image of the Self changed over its course.
