The Portable Jung

What it is

A one-volume anthology of Jung’s own writing, chosen and introduced by Joseph Campbell. It draws on the Collected Works in R. F. C. Hull’s translation and arranges the selections so that they build on each other. It is the most economical way to read Jung in the original.

Who it is for. Readers who have had an introduction and now want primary texts without buying a shelf of volumes.

What is in it

Campbell opens with a biographical sketch and a chronology, then orders fifteen selections in three parts. Part I lays out the structure of the psyche: “The Stages of Life”, “The Structure of the Psyche”, “Instinct and the Unconscious” and “The Concept of the Collective Unconscious”, followed by “The Relations between the Ego and the Unconscious”, the chapters of Aion on the ego, the shadow, and the anima and animus, an essay on marriage, and a long extract from Psychological Types.

Part II shows the ideas at work on particular subjects: “The Transcendent Function”, poetry, the dream series from Psychology and Alchemy, the spiritual problem of modern man, and the difference between Eastern and Western thinking. Part III gives two late works: the short lecture “On Synchronicity” and Answer to Job.

How to read it

Follow Campbell’s order for Part I, which is where the foundations are laid. After that, choose by interest. “The Transcendent Function” and the chapters from Aion repay rereading more than anything else in the volume.

It is still Jung, so it is not easy. Expect long paragraphs, classical references and terms that are defined elsewhere. The glossary in this library can be kept open alongside.

What it leads to

The anthology prints long works at length, so by the end you will have read the second of the Two Essays, the opening of Aion, and Answer to Job. The natural next steps are the complete volumes those pieces come from, starting with The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious.

Key ideas in this book

  • The ego
  • The shadow
  • The anima
  • The transcendent function
  • Psychological types
  • Synchronicity

What to read next

Core works · 1934-1954 The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious Readers who know the outline of Jung’s psychology and want the primary texts on archetypes. Read two or three essays first, not the whole volume. Deep water · 1951 Aion: Researches into the Phenomenology of the Self Everyone should read the first four chapters. The remainder is for readers with a strong interest in religion and symbolism. Core works · 1921 Psychological Types Anyone who came to Jung through personality types and wants to see what he actually wrote. Start with Chapter X.

Questions people ask

Who edited The Portable Jung?

Joseph Campbell, the scholar of mythology, who selected the texts and wrote the introduction. It was published by Viking in 1971.

Is The Portable Jung good for beginners?

It is better as a second book. The selections are Jung’s own technical writing, so most readers do well to read an introduction such as Man and His Symbols first.
