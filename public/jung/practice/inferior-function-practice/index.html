Making room for your inferior function

What it is

A low-pressure way of giving your least developed function a small, regular place in your life. It follows Jung’s advice that the inferior function cannot be forced and has to be approached indirectly. The aim is a working relationship with your weak side. Mastery is not the aim.

Before you start. Work in this area can stir up strong feelings and an old sense of inadequacy. Go slowly and keep the stakes low. If it leads to persistent low mood or distress, pause and talk to a qualified professional. This guide is educational and is not treatment.

The steps

  1. Identify it

    Your inferior function is the opposite of the one you rely on most: feeling for a thinking type, thinking for a feeling type, intuition for a sensation type, sensation for an intuitive. Confirm it by experience. It is the area where you are slowest, most defensive, and most thrown under stress.

  2. Lower the stakes

    Choose a setting where nothing depends on doing well: a hobby, a private notebook, an hour at the weekend. Do not begin at work or in an important relationship. The inferior function behaves like a beginner and needs a beginner’s conditions.

  3. Go by way of the auxiliary

    Jung warned that the leading function will not let its opposite be trained directly. Use your second function as a bridge. A thinking type with good intuition might approach feeling through music or stories. One with good sensation might approach it through a concrete act of care.

  4. Keep it small and regular

    Fifteen minutes, a few times a week, is enough. A thinking type can ask what they actually like and say one appreciation aloud. A feeling type can write out the case for a view they dislike. A sensation type can follow a hunch for an afternoon. An intuitive can do one physical task slowly and completely.

  5. Study the eruptions

    When you are tired or strained and the inferior function takes over in its crude form, do not just recover and forget. Afterward, note what set it off and what it seemed to want. These episodes are the function’s own way of asking for attention.

  6. Let it stay slow

    Resist turning this into a project to be optimized. The inferior function keeps a childlike quality however long you attend to it. Its value lies in the freshness and depth it brings. Efficiency belongs to your dominant function.

Where it comes from in Jung

In Psychological Types Jung explains that because one function is developed at the expense of its opposite, the neglected one remains bound up with the unconscious. He wrote that for the development of character the other side has to be allowed expression, and he also observed that attempts to develop it directly out of the unconscious fail, because the conscious standpoint is violated too abruptly. The route, he said, runs through the auxiliary functions.

The fullest practical discussion is by Marie-Louise von Franz, in her lectures on the inferior function. The examples in the steps above follow her descriptions of how each type meets its weak side.

What it is not

It is not a plan for becoming balanced in the sense of equally good at everything. Jung thought that impossible, and von Franz stressed that the inferior function never comes fully under conscious control. It is also not self-improvement for the sake of performance. The point is contact with a part of yourself that has been left out.

What to expect

Expect to feel foolish. The inferior function is slow and emotional, and time spent in it can feel wasted by the standards of your strong side. Expect touchiness when anyone comments. Over months, the usual signs of progress are indirect: fewer explosive episodes under stress, less irritation at people who lead with that function, and a sense that life has more than one register.

Why it matters

Jung connected the inferior function with the shadow and with the pull toward wholeness that he called individuation. In the second half of life the neglected side tends to demand attention whether it is invited or not. Giving it a modest place beforehand is gentler than waiting for it to force its way in.

Questions people ask

How do you develop the inferior function?

Indirectly and slowly, according to Jung: through your auxiliary functions, in small doses, in situations where nothing important is at stake. He thought direct training tends to fail.

How do I know which function is my inferior?

It is the opposite of your dominant function. In practice it is the area where you feel least competent and most sensitive, and where you tend to overreact when tired or under pressure.

Can the inferior function become strong?

Jung thought it could be brought closer to consciousness and given a place in life. He did not think it would ever match the dominant function in speed or reliability.
