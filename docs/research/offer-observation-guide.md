# Five-person result-to-report observation

Status: prepared; no participants observed yet. Keep the current offer, price and free result unchanged throughout these sessions.

## What this can answer

Observe whether people notice the upgrade, understand what the CA$7 Insight report adds, and find that addition useful. Five sessions can reveal usability problems and reasons to investigate; they cannot estimate conversion uplift or represent all visitors.

Recruit five adults who are interested in understanding their personality and would realistically consider taking this assessment. Include people with different levels of familiarity with cognitive functions. Aim for three phone sessions and two desktop sessions if those resemble the actual audience. Record traffic source and device separately; do not infer source from the internal button they clicked.

Do not contact participants or start recording without their agreement. If compensation is offered, make it independent of their answers or buying decision.

## Before each session

- Obtain consent to observe. Ask separately before recording; notes alone are sufficient. Explain that they can stop or skip personal discussion at any time.
- Ask them to hide notifications and unrelated private tabs. Do not record assessment answers, email addresses, payment details or credentials. Use participant codes P1–P5.
- Allow 35–45 minutes, including the advertised assessment time. Use the same deployed version for all five; record its date/version.
- Use their normal device and a fresh assessment. Do not seed fictional results and call them the participant's result.
- If using the live site, keep a private research-exclusion record with the consented anonymous browser identifier and session time. Exclude those browser journeys from commercial conversion comparisons. Keep participant contact details separate. Never post browser identifiers publicly.
- Do not require a purchase. Stop before entering payment information. If a checkout is opened for research, exclude it from purchase-interest metrics.

## Moderator script

“We are testing the website, not you. There are no right answers. Please use it as you normally would and tell me what you are thinking when that feels comfortable. You do not need to discuss your personal answers or buy anything. You can stop at any time.”

Task: “Take the assessment. Once you reach your result, explore it and decide what you would do next.”

During the first pass, do not mention “Go deeper,” the report, the price, or where to click. Avoid explaining the interface. If they pause, use “What are you thinking?” rather than directing them. Note moderator help separately from spontaneous behavior.

If they would leave, ask: “What brought you to that decision?” Record their answer before showing anything they missed.

After their natural exploration, ask:

1. “What have you learned from this result?”
2. “What options do you think you have from here?”
3. If they noticed the paid report: “In your own words, what would you receive? What does it cost? Is that a one-time payment or ongoing?”
4. “What, if anything, would make that useful to you right now?”
5. “What information is missing or unclear?”
6. “What would you do next if I weren't here?”

Only after the unprompted portion, if they missed the report, ask them to explore “Go deeper.” Label all subsequent observations **prompted**. Ask the same comprehension questions. Do not count this as spontaneous discovery.

Do not substitute “Would you pay?” for behavior. A stated intention is weaker evidence than choosing to open checkout. Neither is a purchase.

## How to interpret the sessions

- If people overlook the upgrade but understand it after a neutral prompt, investigate discovery and navigation.
- If they notice it but cannot explain the paid addition, investigate comprehension.
- If they understand it but do not need it, preserve their stated reason; investigate relevance, trust or timing only when their behavior or words support it.
- If checkout is chosen, separate checkout usability from offer interest. An expired session alone is not a payment failure.
- Report counts as “2 of these 5 participants,” not population conversion rates. Repeated observations suggest the next experiment; they do not prove its effect.

Keep the current offer steady until the sessions are synthesized. Compare future completed-test cohorts with the historical baseline using the same follow-up window and separate external traffic sources. Historical missing attribution remains unknown.
