A dream practice: recording and reading your dreams

What it is

A simple routine for writing dreams down and thinking about them in the order Jung worked: the dream as dreamed, the life context, your own associations, and then the question of what the dream adds to your conscious view. It is a way of paying attention. It does not predict anything.

Before you start. If dream work leaves you more anxious or sleepless, or if you have recurring nightmares connected with trauma, pause and speak with a qualified professional. Do not make a major decision on the strength of a dream alone. This guide is educational and is not treatment.

The steps

  1. Record it at once

    Keep a notebook by the bed. On waking, before you speak or check a screen, write the dream in the present tense with every detail you have, including the feeling. Do not tidy it or explain it. Add the date and a short title.

  2. Note the context

    Under the dream, write two or three lines about the previous day and about what is occupying you at present. Jung held that a dream cannot be read without knowing the conscious situation it answers.

  3. Gather your associations

    Take each main image in turn and ask what it is to you. What is this house, this animal, this person like? Stay close to the image and return to it after each answer. Jung warned against chains of free association that wander away from the dream.

  4. Ask the compensation question

    Ask which conscious attitude this dream answers. What does it show that I have been leaving out, overdoing or underrating? Write one tentative sentence. Do not force an answer.

  5. Try both levels

    Read the dream once as being about the actual people and events in it. Then read it again as if every figure were a part of you. Jung found the second reading more often fruitful, especially when the figures are strangers or the feeling is out of scale.

  6. Watch the series

    Reread your dreams once a month. Recurring places, figures and problems matter more than any single dream, and a later dream often corrects a wrong reading of an earlier one.

Where it comes from in Jung

Jung regarded dreams as natural products of the psyche that mean what they say, in a language of images. His working principle was compensation: the dream supplies what the conscious attitude lacks. The order of the steps above follows his own practice of establishing the context and the dreamer’s associations before interpreting anything.

He also said that he attached little weight to the interpretation of a single dream and preferred to work with a series, and that he tried to meet each dream as something unknown instead of applying a theory to it.

What it is not

It is not a dictionary method. Jung rejected fixed meanings for dream images, because the same snake or house means different things to different dreamers. It is not fortune-telling. And a dream is not an instruction. It is one view of the situation, from a side you had not considered, and the conscious mind still has to weigh it.

What to expect

Recall usually improves within a week or two of writing dreams down, simply because you are attending to them. Most dreams will stay partly unexplained, and that is normal. Understanding often arrives later, when a subsequent dream or an event in waking life throws light back on an old entry.

The benefit Jung expected was modest and real: a running correction to one-sidedness, and a growing familiarity with the parts of yourself that do not appear in daylight.

Going further

Images that carry a strong charge can be taken up in active imagination, which Jung considered a more demanding method and did not recommend to everyone. For motifs that feel larger than personal, such as floods, descents or wise figures, the pages on archetypes and symbols give the background.

Questions people ask

How do I start a dream journal?

Keep paper by the bed and write each dream down immediately on waking, in the present tense, with the feeling and the date. Add a line about what was on your mind the day before.

How did Jung say to interpret a dream?

By establishing the dreamer’s situation, collecting personal associations to each image, and asking what conscious attitude the dream compensates. He preferred series of dreams to single ones.

Should I use a dream dictionary?

Jung advised against it. He held that the meaning of an image depends on the individual dreamer and the context, so a fixed list of meanings misleads more than it helps.
