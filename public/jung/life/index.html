The Jung Library

Carl Jung’s life, in 32 dates

Jung said he could only tell his life as a story of inner events. This timeline gives the outer ones: where he was, what he published, and which ideas came out of each period.

In short

Carl Gustav Jung (1875 to 1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist. He worked with Freud from 1907 to 1913, broke with him, and went on to found analytical psychology. His concepts include the collective unconscious, archetypes, the shadow, individuation, and the psychological types that later personality tests were built on.

  1. 26 July 1875Born in Kesswil

    Carl Gustav Jung is born in Kesswil, in the Swiss canton of Thurgau, the son of a country pastor.

  2. 1895Medical studies in Basel

    He begins to study medicine at the University of Basel. His father dies the following year, leaving the family with little money.

  3. December 1900The Burghölzli

    He moves to Zurich and starts work at the Burghölzli psychiatric hospital under Eugen Bleuler.

    • Complexes
  4. 1903Marriage to Emma Rauschenbach

    He marries Emma Rauschenbach, who later becomes an analyst and author in her own right.

    • The animus
  5. 1906Word association and Freud

    He sends Sigmund Freud a copy of his Studies in Word Association, the research that established the theory of complexes. Their correspondence begins.

    • Complexes
    • The personal unconscious
  6. 3 March 1907First meeting with Freud

    Jung visits Freud in Vienna. By Jung’s account they talked almost without a break for thirteen hours.

  7. 1909Küsnacht and America

    He leaves the Burghölzli for private practice at his home in Küsnacht, and travels with Freud to lecture at Clark University in Massachusetts.

  8. 1910President of the psychoanalytic association

    At Freud’s proposal he becomes president of the newly formed International Psychoanalytical Association.

  9. 1912The book that divided them

    He publishes the work later revised as Symbols of Transformation, in which libido is treated as general psychic energy. It leads to the break with Freud.

    • Libido as psychic energy
    • The hero
  10. September 1913Break with Freud

    The two men meet for the last time at the psychoanalytic congress in Munich, where Jung lectures on psychological types. A period of isolation follows.

    • Psychological types
    • Introversion and extraversion
  11. 12 November 1913The inner experiment begins

    He starts the notebooks now known as the Black Books, recording the fantasies he later called his confrontation with the unconscious.

    • Active imagination
  12. 1916Seven Sermons and the first mandala

    He prints the Seven Sermons to the Dead privately and paints his first mandala. The essay “The Transcendent Function” also dates from this year.

    • The mandala
    • The transcendent function
  13. 1918The daily mandalas

    While commanding a camp for interned British soldiers at Château d’Oex in 1918 and 1919, he sketches a mandala each morning and arrives at his idea of the Self.

    • The mandala
    • The Self
  14. 1921Psychological Types

    He publishes Psychological Types, introducing introversion, extraversion and the four functions. The English translation follows in 1923.

    • Psychological types
    • The four functions
    • The inferior function
  15. 1923The tower at Bollingen

    On land bought the year before, he builds a round stone tower on the upper lake of Zurich. He adds to it over the next three decades.

    • The Self
  16. 1925Journeys to New Mexico and East Africa

    After visiting Taos Pueblo in the winter of 1924 to 1925, he sets out in October 1925 on an expedition to East Africa.

    • The collective unconscious
  17. 1928The Golden Flower and the turn to alchemy

    Richard Wilhelm sends him the Chinese text The Secret of the Golden Flower. Jung’s commentary appears with Wilhelm’s translation in 1929, and his long study of alchemy begins.

    • Jung and alchemy
    • The mandala
  18. 1930The Red Book set aside

    After some sixteen years he stops work on the illuminated manuscript of The Red Book, leaving it unfinished.

    • Active imagination
  19. 1933Eranos, and a contested presidency

    The Eranos meetings begin at Ascona, where he lectures for many years. In the same year he becomes president of the General Medical Society for Psychotherapy. His conduct in that role and remarks he published in 1934 have been criticized ever since.

  20. 1937Yale and India

    He gives the Terry Lectures at Yale, published as Psychology and Religion, and travels to India in 1937 and 1938.

    • The numinous
  21. 1944Illness and Psychology and Alchemy

    He suffers a heart attack and resigns the professorship at Basel that he had taken up the year before. Psychology and Alchemy is published.

    • Jung and alchemy
  22. 1948The C. G. Jung Institute

    The C. G. Jung Institute is founded in Zurich to train analysts and carry on research.

  23. 1951Aion

    He publishes Aion, with his final definitions of the ego, the shadow, the anima and animus, and the Self.

    • The Self
    • The shadow
    • The anima
  24. 1952Answer to Job and synchronicity

    Answer to Job appears, and his essay on synchronicity is published alongside a study by the physicist Wolfgang Pauli.

    • Synchronicity
    • The tension of opposites
  25. 27 November 1955Death of Emma Jung

    His wife Emma dies after fifty-two years of marriage. The first part of Mysterium Coniunctionis, his last major work, is published the same year.

    • Jung and alchemy
    • The tension of opposites
  26. 1957The Undiscovered Self

    He publishes The Undiscovered Self. Work is also under way with Aniela Jaffé on the memoir that the publisher Kurt Wolff had proposed the year before.

    • The shadow
    • Projection
  27. 1959Face to Face

    The BBC broadcasts John Freeman’s interview with him, filmed at his home. Asked whether he believes in God, he answers that he does not need to believe.

    • The numinous
  28. 6 June 1961Death at Küsnacht

    He dies at home after a short illness, shortly after finishing his essay for Man and His Symbols.

    • Dreams
    • Symbols
  29. 1962Memories, Dreams, Reflections

    The memoir recorded and edited by Aniela Jaffé is published in German. The English translation follows in 1963.

    • Individuation
  30. 1964Man and His Symbols

    The illustrated introduction he planned for general readers is published, with his own essay and four parts by colleagues.

    • Symbols
    • Archetypes
  31. October 2009The Red Book is published

    Nearly eighty years after he set it aside, The Red Book is published in facsimile with a translation, edited by Sonu Shamdasani.

    • Active imagination
    • The wise old man
  32. 2020The Black Books are published

    The seven notebooks in which he first recorded his fantasies, kept mainly between 1913 and 1932, are published.

    • Active imagination

A note on the 1930s

Jung’s conduct in the first years of Nazi rule remains disputed. In 1933 he accepted the presidency of a German-based psychotherapy society and edited its journal, and in 1934 he published remarks contrasting “Aryan” and “Jewish” psychology that have been widely condemned. His defenders point to the help he gave Jewish colleagues and to his later criticism of National Socialism. A fair reading of his work does not require settling the question, but it should not leave it out.
