The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious

What it is

Volume 9, part 1 of the Collected Works: Jung’s essays on the archetypes, written between 1934 and 1954. It contains his definitions of the collective unconscious and his studies of particular figures, including the mother, the child, the trickster and the wise old man, and it ends with his work on mandalas.

Who it is for. Readers who know the outline of Jung’s psychology and want the primary texts on archetypes. Read two or three essays first, not the whole volume.

What is in it

The volume is a collection, not a continuous book. Three essays at the front state the theory: “Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious”, “The Concept of the Collective Unconscious”, and “Concerning the Archetypes, with Special Reference to the Anima Concept”.

The middle section takes single archetypes in turn: the mother, rebirth, the child, the Kore or maiden, the spirit in fairy tales, where the wise old man appears, and the trickster. The last section turns to individuation, with a long case study told through a patient’s paintings and the essay “Concerning Mandala Symbolism”, illustrated with plates.

How to read it

Start with “The Concept of the Collective Unconscious”. It is a lecture of about a dozen pages and the clearest statement he made. Then read “Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious”, which moves through the shadow, the anima and the old man as a sequence. After those two, pick essays by interest. The one on the mother archetype and the one on the child are the richest.

Expect a great deal of mythological comparison. Jung’s method is to pile up parallels until the pattern shows, which can be tiring. It is fine to skim the examples and hold on to the argument.

What it leads to

Its companion is part 2 of the same volume, Aion, which treats the Self. Symbols of Transformation is the early, sprawling version of the same material. For the mandala and its link to alchemy, continue with Psychology and Alchemy.

What to read next

Deep water · 1951 Aion: Researches into the Phenomenology of the Self Everyone should read the first four chapters. The remainder is for readers with a strong interest in religion and symbolism. Deep water · 1912/1952 Symbols of Transformation Readers interested in mythology and in the origins of Jung’s ideas. It is not a first book, and it is not his clearest. Deep water · 1944 Psychology and Alchemy Readers who have the basics and are curious about why alchemy mattered so much to Jung. The introduction can be read by anyone.

Questions people ask

Which essay on archetypes should I read first?

“The Concept of the Collective Unconscious” (1936). It is short, was written as a lecture, and defines the idea plainly. Follow it with “Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious”.

Does this book list the twelve archetypes?

No. Jung never gave a list of twelve. This volume discusses several archetypal figures and states that their number cannot be fixed.
