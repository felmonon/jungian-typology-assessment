Modern Man in Search of a Soul

What it is

A collection of eleven essays and lectures from around 1930, published in English in 1933. It is Jung speaking to educated general audiences about dreams, therapy, the stages of life, his differences with Freud, and the spiritual condition of modern people. It is short, direct and quotable.

Who it is for. Readers who prefer essays to a system, and anyone curious about Jung’s view of meaning, religion and midlife.

What is in it

The book was assembled for English readers and translated by W. S. Dell and Cary F. Baynes. Most of the pieces began as lectures, which is why they read easily. The practical essays come first: on the use of dream analysis, on the problems and aims of psychotherapy, and a compact statement of his theory of types.

The second half widens out. “The Stages of Life” compares a life to the course of the sun and argues that the afternoon cannot be lived by the rules of the morning. “Freud and Jung: Contrasts” is his own short account of the disagreement. “Archaic Man”, “The Spiritual Problem of Modern Man” and “Psychotherapists or the Clergy” carry his claim that many modern troubles are troubles of meaning.

How to read it

The essays stand alone, so read in any order. Good first choices are “The Stages of Life”, “The Aims of Psychotherapy” and “Freud and Jung: Contrasts”. Keep the date in mind. Several passages about “primitive” peoples and about national character reflect the assumptions of European writers of the 1920s and read badly now.

What it leads to

This is the book for Jung on dreams as honest statements, on the second half of life, and on the practical spirit of his therapy: his refusal to force one method on every patient. Most of its essays were later retranslated for the Collected Works. If the themes of meaning and society interest you, The Undiscovered Self continues them twenty-five years on. For the structure of his psychology, go next to the Two Essays.

Key ideas in this book

  • Dreams
  • Individuation
  • Psychological types
  • Libido as psychic energy
  • The numinous

What to read next

Start here · 1957 The Undiscovered Self Readers interested in Jung on politics, conformity and religion, and anyone who wants his late thought in a hundred pages. Core works · 1917/1928 Two Essays on Analytical Psychology Readers who want the theory in Jung’s own words and in order. It is widely regarded as the best systematic introduction he wrote. Start here · 1962 Memories, Dreams, Reflections Anyone who wants to know who Jung was before learning what he thought. It makes the theory make sense, because it shows what the theory was built to explain.

Questions people ask

What is Modern Man in Search of a Soul about?

It is a set of eleven essays on dream analysis, psychotherapy, psychological types, the stages of life, Jung’s differences with Freud, and the loss of meaning in modern life.

Is Modern Man in Search of a Soul hard to read?

No. Most of it began as public lectures. It is among the most approachable books of Jung’s own writing.
