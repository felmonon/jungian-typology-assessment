How the psyche moves

Enantiodromia

The tendency of any extreme to turn, in time, into its opposite.

Also searched as: running counter to

In short

Enantiodromia means running counter to. Jung took the word from accounts of the Greek philosopher Heraclitus and used it for a psychological law: an extreme, one-sided attitude builds up its opposite in the unconscious, which eventually breaks through and takes over. The persecutor becomes the convert, the rationalist the believer.

What Jung meant

Heraclitus held that everything flows into its opposite. Jung applied the thought to the psyche and connected it to compensation. A moderate one-sidedness is corrected quietly. An extreme one is not corrected. It is overturned. The energy held back by the conscious attitude accumulates on the other side until the balance tips. In the Two Essays he credited Heraclitus with discovering this regulating function of opposites: sooner or later everything runs into its opposite.

How it shows up

His own examples are conversions: Saul the persecutor becoming Paul the apostle, and Swedenborg turning from scholar to seer. On a small scale it is the strict dieter who binges, the tireless helper who turns bitter, the skeptic swept into a belief. Jung saw the same swing in history, when an age of reason produces an eruption of the irrational. The reversal is usually experienced as something that happens to a person, since the new position was prepared out of sight. The clue beforehand is rigidity: the more absolute and effortful the conscious position, the closer the turn usually is.

Reversal or integration

Jung did not present the swing as growth. A person who flips from one extreme to the other is as one-sided as before, only facing the other way, and the old attitude now sits in the unconscious waiting its turn. What he valued was the alternative: becoming conscious of the opposite before it takes over, and holding both. That is the work he described under the tension of opposites and the transcendent function.

Why it matters

The idea is a warning and a way of reading extremes. Any position held with absolute certainty, he suggested, is suspect, because the certainty is partly a defense against the opposite. Midlife was for him the classic moment of enantiodromia, when the values of the first half of life begin to reverse. Knowing the pattern makes it possible to meet the reversal with some awareness instead of being carried by it.

Common misreadings

  • “Enantiodromia is balance being restored.” It is an overshoot. One extreme replaces another, and nothing has been balanced.
  • “It is the same as karma or a law of history.” Jung meant a psychological regularity observed in individuals and groups, not a cosmic rule.
  • “Jung coined the word.” He credited Heraclitus. The term comes from later Greek summaries of his thought.

Questions people ask

What does enantiodromia mean?

Literally “running counter to”. Jung used it for the tendency of an extreme conscious attitude to be replaced by its unconscious opposite.

What is an example of enantiodromia?

Jung cited the conversion of Saul into Paul. Everyday versions include the workaholic who abruptly quits everything or the rigid moralist who is caught in a scandal.

How do you avoid enantiodromia?

Jung’s answer was awareness of the opposite. A position that admits its other side is less likely to be overturned by it.
