Two Essays on Analytical Psychology

What it is

Volume 7 of the Collected Works: the two essays in which Jung first set out his psychology as a whole. One distinguishes his view from Freud’s and Adler’s and introduces the collective unconscious. The other follows what happens when a person begins to meet the unconscious, stage by stage.

Who it is for. Readers who want the theory in Jung’s own words and in order. It is widely regarded as the best systematic introduction he wrote.

What is in it

The first essay, “On the Psychology of the Unconscious”, goes back to a paper of 1912, became a small book in 1917, and was revised several times until 1943. Jung takes one case and reads it first as Freud would, in terms of sexuality, and then as Adler would, in terms of power. He argues that both are right and that the difference lies in the type of the theorist. From there he introduces the two attitudes, the personal and the collective unconscious, and the archetypes.

The second essay, “The Relations between the Ego and the Unconscious” (1928), is the core. Its first part describes the effects of the unconscious on consciousness, including the persona and inflation. Its second part describes individuation: the encounter with the anima and animus, the technique of telling oneself apart from these figures, and the danger he calls the mana-personality.

How to read it

Many readers do better starting with the second essay, which is clearer and more original, and returning to the first for the background. Read slowly. Jung illustrates each step with case material, and the cases are where the abstractions become visible. The appendices hold the earliest versions of both essays and can be skipped.

What it leads to

Almost every later book enlarges something sketched here. The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious fills in the figures. Aion restates the structure in its final form. Psychological Types develops the passage on attitudes into a full theory. If you read only one volume of the Collected Works, this is the one most often recommended.

Key ideas in this book

  • The persona
  • The anima
  • The animus
  • Individuation
  • Inflation
  • The collective unconscious

What to read next

Core works · 1934-1954 The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious Readers who know the outline of Jung’s psychology and want the primary texts on archetypes. Read two or three essays first, not the whole volume. Deep water · 1951 Aion: Researches into the Phenomenology of the Self Everyone should read the first four chapters. The remainder is for readers with a strong interest in religion and symbolism. Core works · 1921 Psychological Types Anyone who came to Jung through personality types and wants to see what he actually wrote. Start with Chapter X.

Questions people ask

What are the two essays in Two Essays on Analytical Psychology?

“On the Psychology of the Unconscious” and “The Relations between the Ego and the Unconscious”. Together they form volume 7 of Jung’s Collected Works.

Which volume of the Collected Works should I read first?

Volume 7 is the usual recommendation, because it presents the whole of Jung’s psychology in a connected way and at moderate length.
