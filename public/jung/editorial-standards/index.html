The Jung Library

Editorial standards

The library exists so that a reader can find out what Jung actually meant, in plain words, and check it. These are the rules it is written to.

Quotations

  • Every quotation is checked. A line is quoted only after its wording and source have been seen at a published source. Quote-card sites and unsourced lists do not count.
  • Citations are specific. We cite the work and the essay or chapter. Collected Works references give the volume and, where we could confirm it, the paragraph number. “CW 9ii, §14” means volume 9, part ii, paragraph 14. Where a paragraph could not be confirmed, it is left out. We do not guess.
  • What cannot be verified is paraphrased. If we cannot confirm the wording, we describe the idea in our own words, without quotation marks, and say which work it comes from.
  • Quotations are short. Jung’s works are in copyright. We quote briefly, for the purpose of commentary, and point to where the full passage can be read.
  • Misquotations are labeled. The quotes page separates verified lines from paraphrases, misattributions and lines with no known source.

Interpretation

  • We attribute. Entries say “Jung held” or “in Jung’s account”. His theories are presented as his theories. They are not presented as established science, and where mainstream psychology disagrees, the entry says so.
  • We separate Jung from his successors. Ideas that come from Marie-Louise von Franz, Erich Neumann, James Hillman, Joseph Campbell, or Briggs and Myers are credited to them.
  • We name what has dated. Parts of Jung’s writing on gender, on race and culture, and on his own role in the 1930s are contested or plainly of their time. The relevant entries say so.

What we do not claim

  • TypeJung is an independent project. It is not affiliated with the Jung family, the Foundation of the Works of C. G. Jung, the C. G. Jung Institute, or any professional association of analysts.
  • The library is not written by licensed analysts and is not a substitute for analysis, therapy or medical care. Practice pages say when to stop and when to seek professional support.
  • The free map on this site is a self-report tool built on Jung’s typology. It suggests a likely dominant and inferior function. It does not measure the shadow, archetypes, complexes or anything else described in these pages.

How entries are written

Entries are drafted with the help of AI tools. Every quotation and citation is then checked against a published source before it goes live, dates and publication facts are checked against reference works, and anything that cannot be confirmed is cut or rewritten as a paraphrase. An automated check runs on every build and rejects any quotation that has no recorded source. We would rather publish a shorter entry than an unverified one.

Corrections

We will get things wrong. If you find a misquotation, a wrong citation, a factual error or a reading you think is unfair to Jung, write to support@typejung.com with the page and the source. Confirmed errors are corrected.

Sources we rely on

Jung’s Collected Works (Princeton University Press and Routledge), his published letters and Memories, Dreams, Reflections; Daryl Sharp’s Jung Lexicon; the Psychology Club Zurich’s published terms; the sourced sections of Wikiquote; Quote Investigator; and standard reference works for dates and publication facts.
