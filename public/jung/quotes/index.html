The Jung Library

Carl Jung quotes, with sources

46 quotations checked against published sources, each cited to the work it comes from. Below them, 12 popular lines that are paraphrased, misattributed or have no source, with what he actually wrote.

On the shadow and self-knowledge

Verified · 1945
One does not become enlightened by imagining figures of light, but by making the darkness conscious.
C. G. Jung, “The Philosophical Tree”, Alchemical Studies (CW 13), §335

From a comment on a patient’s painting. Jung is criticizing a spirituality that fills the mind with ideal images and avoids the shadow. He adds that the second procedure is disagreeable and therefore not popular.

  • The shadow
  • Individuation
Verified · 1951
The shadow is a moral problem that challenges the whole ego-personality, for no one can become conscious of the shadow without considerable moral effort.
C. G. Jung, “The Shadow”, Aion (CW 9ii), §14

The opening of his late chapter on the shadow. The word moral is deliberate: seeing the shadow is a question of honesty before it is one of technique.

  • The shadow
Verified · 1944
People will do anything, no matter how absurd, in order to avoid facing their own souls.
C. G. Jung, “Individual Dream Symbolism in Relation to Alchemy”, Psychology and Alchemy (CW 12), §126

He goes on to list the evasions: exercises, strict diets, texts learned by heart. All of them, he says, because people do not believe anything useful could come out of their own souls.

  • The shadow
  • Individuation
Verified · 1962
Everything that irritates us about others can lead us to an understanding of ourselves.
C. G. Jung, “Travels”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, p. 246

The context is national character. Jung says he understood his own country only by seeing where he did not fit in elsewhere. Applying the line to personal irritation is a fair extension, and matches what he wrote about projection.

  • Projection
  • The shadow
Verified · 1951
Projections change the world into the replica of one’s own unknown face.
C. G. Jung, “The Shadow”, Aion (CW 9ii), §17

His description of what unrecognized projections do: a person ends up relating to their own unseen qualities and becomes isolated from other people as they are.

  • Projection
  • The shadow
Verified · 1951
The psychological rule says that when an inner situation is not made conscious, it happens outside, as fate.
C. G. Jung, “Christ, a Symbol of the Self”, Aion (CW 9ii), §126

The real sentence behind the most shared Jung quotation on the internet. It continues: when a person does not become conscious of their inner opposite, the world must act out the conflict.

  • The tension of opposites
  • Projection
Verified · 1934
Everyone knows nowadays that people “have complexes.” What is not so well known, though far more important theoretically, is that complexes can have us.
C. G. Jung, “A Review of the Complex Theory”, The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche (CW 8), §200

From his late summary of the theory that made his name. The point is the autonomy of a complex: it can take over a reaction before the ego has a say.

  • Complexes
Verified · 1943
A complex becomes pathological only when we think we have not got it.
C. G. Jung, “Psychotherapy and a Philosophy of Life”, The Practice of Psychotherapy (CW 16), §179

Jung considered complexes normal. The trouble, in his view, starts with the belief that one is free of them.

  • Complexes
  • The shadow

On individuation and the Self

Verified · 1916
Your vision will become clear only when you can look into your own heart. Without, everything seems discordant; only within does it coalesce into unity. Who looks outside dreams; who looks inside awakes.
C. G. Jung, Letter to Fanny Bowditch, 22 October 1916, C. G. Jung Letters, vol. 1, p. 33

Written to a patient during the First World War. The last sentence is usually quoted alone.

  • Individuation
  • The Self
Verified · 1947
Individuation does not shut one out from the world, but gathers the world to itself.
C. G. Jung, “On the Nature of the Psyche”, The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche (CW 8), §432

His answer to the charge that individuation is self-absorption. In the same passage he complains that it is constantly confused with mere ego-centeredness.

  • Individuation
Verified · 1946
The goal is important only as an idea; the essential thing is the opus which leads to the goal: that is the goal of a lifetime.
C. G. Jung, “The Psychology of the Transference”, The Practice of Psychotherapy (CW 16), §400

Opus is the alchemists’ word for their work. Jung did not think anyone finishes individuating. The value lies in the work.

  • Individuation
  • Jung and alchemy
Verified · 1944
The self is not only the centre, but also the whole circumference which embraces both conscious and unconscious; it is the centre of this totality, just as the ego is the centre of consciousness.
C. G. Jung, “Introduction”, Psychology and Alchemy (CW 12), §44

The clearest single statement of the difference between the ego and the Self.

  • The Self
  • The ego
Verified · 1962
As far as we can discern, the sole purpose of human existence is to kindle a light in the darkness of mere being.
C. G. Jung, “On Life after Death”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, p. 326

From the late, speculative part of the memoir. By light he means consciousness.

  • Individuation
  • The Self
Verified · 1962
Life has always seemed to me like a plant that lives on its rhizome. Its true life is invisible, hidden in the rhizome. The part that appears above ground lasts only a single summer. … What we see is the blossom, which passes. The rhizome remains.
C. G. Jung, “Prologue”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections

The image with which he opens the memoir: a single life as the visible flowering of something that lasts longer.

  • The Self
  • The collective unconscious
Verified · 1962
I sketched every morning in a notebook a small circular drawing, a mandala, which seemed to correspond to my inner situation at the time.
C. G. Jung, “Confrontation with the Unconscious”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, ch. VI

He is describing 1918 and 1919. The drawings led him to the idea of the Self as the center of the personality.

  • The mandala
  • The Self

On dreams and the unconscious

Verified · 1934
The dream is the small hidden door in the deepest and most intimate sanctum of the soul, which opens to that primeval cosmic night that was soul long before there was conscious ego and will be soul far beyond what a conscious ego could ever reach.
C. G. Jung, “The Meaning of Psychology for Modern Man”, Civilization in Transition (CW 10)

Jung at his most lyrical about dreams. In practice he read them soberly, image by image, against the dreamer’s situation.

  • Dreams
  • The collective unconscious
Verified · 1926
Dreams are neither deliberate nor arbitrary fabrications; they are natural phenomena which are nothing other than what they pretend to be. They do not deceive, they do not lie, they do not distort or disguise.
C. G. Jung, “Analytical Psychology and Education”, The Development of Personality (CW 17), §189

The sentence that separates him from Freud, who held that a dream disguises its meaning.

  • Dreams
Verified · 1928
…a dream is a theatre in which the dreamer is himself the scene, the player, the prompter, the producer, the author, the public, and the critic.
C. G. Jung, “General Aspects of Dream Psychology”, The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche (CW 8), §509

The basis of reading a dream on the subjective level, where every figure is taken as a part of the dreamer.

  • Dreams
Verified · 1914
The psyche does not merely react, it gives its own specific answer to the influences at work upon it.
C. G. Jung, “Some Crucial Points in Psychoanalysis”, Freud and Psychoanalysis (CW 4), §665

An early statement of a lifelong conviction: a person cannot be explained by causes alone.

  • The psyche
Verified · 1936
It is not . . . a question of inherited ideas but of inherited possibilities of ideas.
C. G. Jung, “Concerning the Archetypes and the Anima Concept”, The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (CW 9i), §136

His standing correction to the commonest misreading of the collective unconscious. Nobody inherits images. People inherit the readiness to form them.

  • Archetypes
  • The collective unconscious
Verified · 1934
In all chaos there is a cosmos, in all disorder a secret order.
C. G. Jung, “Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious”, The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (CW 9i), p. 32

Said of the anima, who seems at first to be pure caprice and turns out to carry a hidden meaning.

  • The anima
  • Archetypes

On conflict, feeling and consciousness

Verified · 1917
Where love reigns, there is no will to power; and where the will to power is paramount, love is lacking. The one is but the shadow of the other
C. G. Jung, “The Problem of the Attitude-Type”, Two Essays on Analytical Psychology (CW 7), §78

From his comparison of Freud’s theory, built on love, with Adler’s, built on power. An older translation, also in circulation, begins “Where love rules”.

  • The tension of opposites
  • The shadow
Verified · 1925
Seldom, or perhaps never, does a marriage develop into an individual relationship smoothly and without crises; there is no coming to consciousness without pain.
C. G. Jung, “Marriage as a Psychological Relationship”, The Development of Personality (CW 17), §331

The famous clause is the second half of a sentence about marriage. It is often printed as if it stood alone or belonged to a different passage.

  • Individuation
  • The tension of opposites
Verified · 1938
…emotion is the chief source of consciousness. There is no change from darkness to light or from inertia to movement without emotion.
C. G. Jung, “Psychological Aspects of the Mother Archetype”, The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (CW 9i), §179

Jung valued conflict because it generates feeling, and feeling is what forces a person to notice.

  • The tension of opposites
  • Complexes
Verified · 1938
There is no consciousness without discrimination of opposites.
C. G. Jung, “Psychological Aspects of the Mother Archetype”, The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (CW 9i), §178

The principle under his whole psychology: to be conscious of anything is to tell it from what it is not.

  • The tension of opposites
Verified · 1929
…the greatest and most important problems of life are all in a certain sense insoluble. … They can never be solved, but only outgrown.
C. G. Jung, Commentary on “The Secret of the Golden Flower”, Alchemical Studies (CW 13)

An observation from his practice: some patients did not solve their problem. A wider interest arose, and the problem lost its urgency.

  • The transcendent function
  • The tension of opposites
Verified · 1916
Man needs difficulties; they are necessary for health.
C. G. Jung, “The Transcendent Function”, The Structure and Dynamics of the Psyche (CW 8)

From the essay in which he sets out how a conflict, held consciously, produces something new.

  • The transcendent function
  • Compensation
Verified · 1962
The pendulum of the mind oscillates between sense and nonsense, not between right and wrong.
C. G. Jung, “Sigmund Freud”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, p. 154

From his reflections on Freud. He goes on to say that numinous experience is dangerous because it lures people to extremes.

  • Enantiodromia
  • The numinous

On relationships and how to live

Verified · 1933
The meeting of two personalities is like the contact of two chemical substances: if there is any reaction, both are transformed.
C. G. Jung, “Problems of Modern Psychotherapy”, Modern Man in Search of a Soul, p. 49

He is writing about doctor and patient. His point is that the doctor is changed by the treatment too, and cannot stand outside it.

  • The wounded healer
  • Projection
Verified · 1933
The shoe that fits one person pinches another; there is no recipe for living that suits all cases.
C. G. Jung, “The Aims of Psychotherapy”, Modern Man in Search of a Soul, p. 69

His reason for refusing to apply a single method to every patient, his own included.

  • Individuation
  • Psychological types
Verified · 1933
The least of things with a meaning is worth more in life than the greatest of things without it.
C. G. Jung, “The Aims of Psychotherapy”, Modern Man in Search of a Soul, p. 67

From the same essay, on patients who are not ill in any definable way and suffer from the emptiness of their lives.

  • Individuation
  • The numinous
Verified · 1939
If there is anything that we wish to change in the child, we should first examine it and see whether it is not something that could better be changed in ourselves.
C. G. Jung, The Integration of the Personality (1939), p. 285

A direct application of what he taught about projection, addressed to parents and teachers.

  • Projection
  • The shadow
Verified · 1962
Loneliness does not come from having no people about one, but from being unable to communicate the things that seem important to oneself, or from holding certain views which others find inadmissible.
C. G. Jung, “Retrospect”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, p. 356

From the last pages of the memoir, where he looks back on a life spent with experiences he could not share.

  • Individuation
Verified · 1960
Even a happy life cannot be without a measure of darkness, and the word “happy” would lose its meaning if it were not balanced by sadness.
C. G. Jung, “The Art of Living”, interview with Gordon Young, C. G. Jung Speaking, pp. 451-452

Said in a newspaper interview a year before his death. The thought is his principle of opposites in everyday words.

  • The tension of opposites
Verified · 1928
A man cannot get rid of himself in favour of an artificial personality without punishment.
C. G. Jung, “Anima and Animus”, Two Essays on Analytical Psychology (CW 7), §307

On identifying with the persona. The punishment he lists is ordinary: bad moods, phobias, compulsions, a private life at odds with the public one.

  • The persona
Verified · 1928
Learn your theories as well as you can, but put them aside when you touch the miracle of the living soul.
C. G. Jung, Contributions to Analytical Psychology (1928), p. 361

Advice to beginning therapists, and a fair warning to anyone reading a library of his concepts.

  • Psychological types
  • Dreams
Verified · 1951
…it is his own hurt that gives the measure of his power to heal. This, and nothing else, is the meaning of the Greek myth of the wounded physician.
C. G. Jung, “Fundamental Questions of Psychotherapy”, The Practice of Psychotherapy (CW 16), §239

The source of the idea of the wounded healer. He means a wound the helper has examined, since the same passage says half of every treatment is the doctor examining himself.

  • The wounded healer

On psychological types

Verified · 1931
Sensation establishes what is actually present, thinking enables us to recognize its meaning, feeling tells us its value, and intuition points to possibilities as to whence it came and whither it is going in a given situation.
C. G. Jung, “A Psychological Theory of Types”, Psychological Types (CW 6), §958

His one-sentence summary of the four functions.

  • The four functions
  • Psychological types
Verified · 1940
The inferior function is practically identical with the dark side of the human personality.
C. G. Jung, “Concerning Rebirth”, The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (CW 9i), §222

The link between his typology and the shadow: what a person has developed least is where the unconscious comes through.

  • The inferior function
  • The shadow
Verified · 1936
His retreat into himself is not a final renunciation of the world, but a search for quietude, where alone it is possible for him to make his contribution to the life of the community.
C. G. Jung, “Psychological Typology”, Psychological Types (CW 6), §979

Jung defending the introvert against the assumption, already common in his day, that turning inward is a failure to adapt.

  • Introversion and extraversion

On society, religion and his own work

Verified · 1957
The bigger the crowd, the more negligible the individual.
C. G. Jung, The Undiscovered Self (in CW 10), p. 14

From his Cold War essay on mass society. His worry was what happens to responsibility when people think of themselves as units.

  • Individuation
  • The collective unconscious
Verified · 1957
You can take away a man’s gods, but only to give him others in return.
C. G. Jung, The Undiscovered Self (in CW 10), p. 63

He held that the religious function does not vanish with belief. It attaches to something else, often the state or an ideology.

  • Archetypes
  • The numinous
Verified · 1942
Freedom stretches only as far as the limits of our consciousness.
C. G. Jung, “Paracelsus the Physician”, The Spirit in Man, Art, and Literature (CW 15)

The end of a passage arguing that nobody is immune to the spirit of their own time.

  • Individuation
  • The collective unconscious
Verified · 1945
…the main interest of my work is not concerned with the treatment of neuroses but rather with the approach to the numinous.
C. G. Jung, Letter to P. W. Martin, 20 August 1945, C. G. Jung Letters, vol. 1, p. 377

A private statement of what he thought he was doing. He adds that the approach to the numinous is the real therapy.

  • The numinous
Verified · 1959
I do not need to believe. I know.
C. G. Jung, BBC television interview “Face to Face”, C. G. Jung Speaking

His answer when John Freeman asked whether he believed in God. The remark caused a stir, and he explained afterward that he was speaking of experience and making no claim of doctrine.

  • The numinous
Verified · 1962
The experiences of the alchemists were, in a sense, my experiences, and their world was my world.
C. G. Jung, “The Work”, Memories, Dreams, Reflections, ch. VII

On discovering that alchemical texts described, in their own language, what he had met in himself and his patients.

Did Jung really say that?

Jung is one of the most misquoted writers on the internet. A line counts as verified here only when the wording and the source have been seen in print. The entries below did not pass that test.

Paraphrases: the idea is his, the wording is not

Paraphrase
Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate.

What Jung wrote

The psychological rule says that when an inner situation is not made conscious, it happens outside, as fate.
C. G. Jung, “Christ, a Symbol of the Self”, Aion (CW 9ii), §126

The most widely shared “Jung quote” is a paraphrase. The idea is his. The wording is not, which is why it never comes with a citation.

  • The tension of opposites
  • Projection
Paraphrase
Thinking is difficult, that’s why most people judge.

What Jung wrote

Thinking is difficult, therefore let the herd pronounce judgment!
C. G. Jung, “Flying Saucers: A Modern Myth”, Civilization in Transition (CW 10), §652

In the original the line is a motto Jung puts in the mouth of the unreflective person who clings to what everyone accepts. The popular version turns a description of an attitude into his own verdict on other people.

  • The shadow
Paraphrase
There is no coming to consciousness without pain. People will do anything, no matter how absurd, in order to avoid facing their own soul. One does not become enlightened by imagining figures of light, but by making the darkness conscious.

What Jung wrote

Seldom, or perhaps never, does a marriage develop into an individual relationship smoothly and without crises; there is no coming to consciousness without pain.
C. G. Jung, “Marriage as a Psychological Relationship”, The Development of Personality (CW 17), §331

Three genuine sentences from three different works, joined into one paragraph: an essay on marriage, Psychology and Alchemy, and “The Philosophical Tree”. Each is real, and each is listed separately on this page. The paragraph is an invention.

  • The shadow
  • Individuation

Misattributed: someone else said it

Misattributed
The privilege of a lifetime is to become who you truly are.

This is Joseph Campbell, who wrote that the privilege of a lifetime is being who you are. It appears in A Joseph Campbell Companion. Campbell admired Jung, which may be how the line migrated.

  • Individuation
Misattributed
You meet your destiny on the road you take to avoid it.

This is Jean de La Fontaine, from the Fables, Book VIII, number 16. It was written two centuries before Jung was born.

  • Enantiodromia
Misattributed
Bidden or not bidden, God is present.

Jung had the Latin, Vocatus atque non vocatus deus aderit, carved over the door of his house, and it is on his gravestone. He did not write it. He found it in Erasmus, who gave it as an ancient Spartan proverb.

  • The numinous

No source found

No source found
I am not what happened to me, I am what I choose to become.

No passage in the Collected Works, the letters or the memoir has been produced for it. It circulates in books by later Jungian writers. What Jung did write, about the ego and the Self, runs the other way: it is not I who create myself, rather I happen to myself (CW 11 §391).

  • Individuation
  • The ego
No source found
Show me a sane man and I will cure him for you.

Not in his writings. Quote Investigator traced it to a remark recalled by his colleague Jolande Jacobi in an interview with the biographer Vincent Brome, so he may well have said something like it in conversation.

  • The shadow
No source found
What you resist persists.

No source in Jung has been found. It is a slogan with a loose family resemblance to his view that what is excluded from consciousness gathers force.

  • Compensation
  • The shadow
No source found
The first half of life is devoted to forming a healthy ego, the second half is going inward and letting go of it.

A summary in words he did not use. Jung did contrast the two halves of life, in “The Stages of Life”. He did not recommend letting go of the ego, which he thought had to stay strong.

  • Individuation
  • The ego
No source found
Whatever is rejected from the self, appears in the world as an event.

No source has been found. It reads like a loose version of the sentence in Aion about an inner situation that happens outside as fate.

  • Projection
  • The tension of opposites
No source found
The deep critical thinker has become the misfit of the world.

Widely shared with his name and never with a source. Wikiquote lists it as attributed only. Nothing in the style or vocabulary suggests Jung.

How these were checked

Wording and citations were checked against Daryl Sharp’s Jung Lexicon, the Psychology Club Zurich’s published list of terms, the sourced section of Wikiquote, Jung’s published letters and memoir, and Quote Investigator. Collected Works references give the volume and, where confirmed, the paragraph (§). Page numbers refer to the editions the sources cite. Quotations are short and are reproduced for commentary.
