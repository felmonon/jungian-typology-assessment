How the psyche moves

Inflation

When the ego swells by identifying with something much larger than itself.

Also searched as: ego inflation, negative inflation

In short

Inflation is Jung’s term for a state in which the ego takes on contents that do not belong to it, usually archetypal ones, and becomes puffed up. The person feels special, chosen or all-knowing, or, in the negative form, uniquely worthless. Either way the ordinary human scale has been lost.

What Jung meant

The word is literal: blown up with air. Jung used it for what happens when the ego identifies with something beyond its limits. The commonest source is contact with the collective unconscious. Archetypal contents carry great energy and a sense of significance. An ego that claims them as personal achievements expands beyond its real size.

He described it as a regression of consciousness into unconsciousness, however elevated it feels. The inflated person is less aware, because they can no longer tell where they end and the archetype begins.

How it shows up

Positive inflation looks like grandiosity: the sense of having seen through everything, of a mission, of being beyond ordinary rules. It often follows a real insight, a spiritual experience or a success. Negative inflation is its mirror: a conviction of being exceptionally bad, guilty or damaged. Jung treated both as the same error, since both make the ego the center of something far larger. Identification with a role, title or office is a milder everyday form, where the persona supplies the borrowed size. Jung noted that the inflated person is usually the last to know. The condition shows in the reactions of other people long before it is felt from inside.

Why it happens

Jung saw inflation as a normal hazard of development, and close to unavoidable when unconscious contents are first assimilated. Each recognition of the shadow or the anima widens the personality, and the widening is easily mistaken for personal greatness. He thought modern people especially prone to it, having lost religious forms that once held these energies outside the ego.

The correction

He did not recommend humility as a pose. The remedy is discrimination: telling apart what belongs to the ego and what belongs to the Self or to humanity at large. Life usually supplies the correction in the form of a fall, the old pattern of pride and collapse. He considered it far better to arrive at the limit by insight.

Common misreadings

  • “Inflation is high self-esteem.” It is a confusion about what one is. Healthy confidence knows its limits.
  • “Only arrogant people get inflated.” Jung described the negative form, feeling uniquely worthless, as inflation too.
  • “Spiritual experiences protect against it.” He thought they were among its most common triggers.

Questions people ask

What is inflation in Jungian psychology?

A state in which the ego identifies with contents larger than itself, typically archetypal ones, and loses its sense of proportion. It can show as grandiosity or as exaggerated worthlessness.

What is negative inflation?

The inverted form: instead of feeling exceptionally great, the person feels exceptionally small, guilty or bad. Jung saw it as the same loss of human scale.
